# Decisions

Choices made where SPEC.md is silent. Newest at the bottom of each milestone.

## Milestone 1: Foundation

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|----------|-----|
| 1 | **Solution layout:** `backend/src/SmoothBudget.Core` (pure money math, no EF or ASP.NET), `backend/src/SmoothBudget.Api` (EF Core, Identity, endpoints), `backend/tests/*`, and `frontend/` (Vite). | Keeps the math unit-testable with no database, and the API is a thin layer over it. |
| 2 | **Money is `long` integer cents** everywhere in storage, API and Core. Fractional factors (split share) are `decimal`, and results round half away from zero to the cent. | The spec says integer cents or decimal. Cents avoid rounding drift in sums. |
| 3 | **Sign convention:** a positive `AmountCents` is money out (a purchase); a negative one is money in (refund, paycheck, transfer in). | This matches Plaid, which keeps the milestone 3 import straightforward. |
| 4 | **Safe-to-spend per day rounds down** to the cent, and is $0 when Left to Spend is zero or negative. Days remaining counts today. | Rounding down never suggests more than is actually safe. |
| 5 | **Status thresholds:** red when Left to Spend < 0; yellow when it is under 15% of total variable limits, or exactly $0; green otherwise. Exactly 15% is green. | The spec gives the 15% rule; the edges are mine. |
| 6 | **"Today" is decided in the household's time zone** (default `America/Denver`, stored on `Household`). | A server running in UTC would otherwise move to the next budget month at 5–6 pm Denver time. |
| 7 | **Budget month label** is the calendar month of its nominal start day ("November 2026" runs 11-06 to 12-06). The start day can be set from 1 to 28. | The spec calls the month "November". Days 29–31 don't exist in every month. |
| 8 | **Uncategorized purchases are not counted** in Left to Spend, but Home shows how many there are ("1 purchase needs a category and isn't counted yet"). | The spec defines the number over variable categories only and says nothing should be silently missing. The real inbox comes in milestone 2. |
| 9 | **Auth:** ASP.NET Core Identity with an HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax cookie (`Secure` outside development), 30-day sliding expiry. Passwords need 10+ characters with upper case, lower case and a digit. Accounts lock for 15 minutes after 5 failed logins. Register and login are rate-limited to 10 requests per minute per IP. | A cookie suits a same-origin PWA and keeps tokens out of JavaScript. |
| 10 | **CSRF:** relies on SameSite=Lax plus JSON-only request bodies (cross-site forms can't send `application/json` without a CORS preflight, and no CORS is enabled). | Simplest adequate protection for a same-origin app. Revisit if the API is ever called cross-origin. |
| 11 | **Household model:** every user belongs to one `Household`, and all financial data hangs off `HouseholdId`. The household id is carried as a claim in the auth cookie. | Lets the partner get a login later by adding a second user to the same household. |
| 12 | **Registration is open** (anyone who can reach the app can create an account with an empty household of their own). | Simplest for milestone 1. **Revisit before deploying publicly.** The likely choice is to disable sign-up after the first user, or invite-only. |
| 13 | **API routes are prefixed with `/api`** (`/api/auth/login`, `/api/home/summary`), and Vite proxies `/api` to the backend in development. | Keeps the frontend and API on one origin, so cookies just work. |
| 14 | **Seed data is generated relative to today's budget month**, by day offset from the month's start. Only transactions dated on or before today are created, and those dated today or yesterday are pending. The seed command resets only the demo household. | Home always shows a realistic, current number whenever you run it. |
| 15 | **Local secrets** live in the git-ignored `.env` at the repo root, loaded by DotNetEnv at startup (real environment variables win). CI and hosting use plain environment variables. | Follows CLAUDE.md. |
| 16 | **Migrations run on startup in Development only** (`Database:MigrateOnStartup` overrides this). The seed command always migrates first. | Convenient locally without silently migrating production. |
| 17 | **API tests run against real PostgreSQL**: each test run creates a throwaway `smoothbudget_test_<guid>` database and drops it afterwards. CI provides Postgres as a service container. | Tests the real provider, query translation and migrations instead of an in-memory fake. |
| 18 | **Home omits "next payday" and "upcoming bills"** for now. | They depend on the pay engine (milestone 9) and recurring detection (milestone 6). The spec's milestone 1 "done when" covers Left to Spend only. |
| 19 | **PWA:** only a web manifest and mobile meta tags for now. The service worker and offline cache come in milestone 10. | Matches the build plan. |
| 20 | **Bottom nav** shows Home, Budget, Debt, Savings, Payday, More (Bills, Partner and Settings go under More, as the spec allows). Only Home is active. | Seven tabs are too cramped at 375 px. |

## Milestone 2: Data in (CSV import, rules, transfers, inbox)

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|----------|-----|
| 21 | **CSV formats are detected from the header row:** Chase checking/savings (`Details, Posting Date, …, Balance`), Chase credit (`Transaction Date, Post Date, …, Memo`) and Apple Card (`…, Clearing Date, …, Amount (USD), Purchased By`). Columns are matched by name, so extra or reordered columns and Chase's trailing comma are fine. | Tolerant of small export changes. **The layouts come from the banks' standard exports, not from your files.** Check with a real export before relying on it. |
| 22 | **Which date is used:** posting date for Chase checking (it's the only one), and transaction (purchase) date for both cards. | SPEC section 3: "A card purchase counts on the purchase date." |
| 23 | **Sign conversion:** Chase checking and Chase credit amounts are flipped (purchases are negative in those exports); Apple Card amounts are kept (purchases are positive). | Matches the app's convention (decision 3). |
| 24 | **Columns dropped on import:** Apple's "Purchased By" (a person's name), Chase's running Balance, Memo and check number. Any run of 8+ digits in a description is masked to its last four (`••8901`). Account "last 4" accepts exactly four digits. | CLAUDE.md: never store account numbers or other people's names. |
| 25 | **Duplicate detection:** bank CSVs have no transaction ids, so a row's fingerprint is a SHA-256 of date, amount and description. On import, for each fingerprint we only add as many rows as the file has beyond what the account already holds. | Re-importing an overlapping export never duplicates anything, and two identical coffees on the same day are both kept. |
| 26 | **Transfer detection, two passes:** (1) single-row signals: a card's own "Payment" row, Chase `LOAN_PMT` and `ACCT_XFER`, and known descriptions ("Payment to Chase card", "CHASE CREDIT CRD AUTOPAY", "APPLECARD GSBANK", "Online Transfer to/from"); (2) pairing: the same amount out of one account and into another of yours within 5 days, where at least one side already looks like a payment or transfer. Bare "AUTOPAY" is not a signal because utilities use it. | Catches payments whose bank-side description is unknown, without ever pairing an ordinary purchase with a refund. |
| 27 | **The checking side of a card payment goes to the first Debt payment category; the card side has no category.** A move to a savings account goes to the first Savings transfer category when the savings side is imported. All of these are `IsTransfer` and never count as spending. | SPEC section 3: the card payment counts as a debt payment, not new spending. |
| 28 | **Categorization precedence:** your own choice > merchant rule > the bank's category. The bank's category is mapped by a small built-in table ("Food & Drink" and "Restaurants" → Eating out, "Grocery" → Groceries, …) or by an exact name match. Chase checking has no category column, so its purchases go to the inbox until a rule matches. | SPEC section 6.1 step 1, read as layers where later ones win. |
| 29 | **Merchant rules match "merchant contains pattern"**, case-insensitive; the longest pattern wins, then the newest. The pattern starts as the cleaned merchant name and is editable (shorten "KING SOOPERS DENVER CO" to "KING SOOPERS" to match every store). | Simple, predictable, and explainable. |
| 30 | **A new rule also applies to existing transactions** that only had a bank category or none. It never changes transactions you set by hand or transfers. Deleting a rule leaves past transactions as they are. | The spec says "future transactions"; clearing matching inbox items too is what you'd expect after creating a rule. |
| 31 | **Your edits lock a transaction:** after you set a category or the transfer flag, rules and transfer detection never change it again. | Your corrections always win. |
| 32 | **The inbox covers all time**, newest first, and holds anything that affects spending but has no category: purchases, fees and refunds. Income, card payments and transfers never appear. Home's line counts only the current budget month. | Nothing is silently missing. Refunds are included because they reduce a category's spending. |
| 33 | **Every upload is an import batch that can be undone** (deletes exactly the rows it added). Undo doesn't revert transfer flags already set on rows from other imports. | A safe way to back out a wrong file or account. |
| 34 | **Upload limit 5 MB; multipart uploads skip ASP.NET's antiforgery token.** The SameSite=Lax auth cookie isn't sent on cross-site POSTs, so a forged upload arrives unauthenticated. | Same reasoning as decision 10. |
| 35 | **Navigation uses the URL hash** (`#/`, `#/budget`, `#/more`) instead of a router library. | Three screens don't need a dependency. |

## Milestone 3: Plaid (Link, cursor sync, webhooks, pending to posted)

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|----------|-----|
| 45 | **Plaid is called through a small hand-written client** (`PlaidClient`) for the endpoints the app uses. The client id and secret go in the `PLAID-CLIENT-ID` / `PLAID-SECRET` headers. Request bodies (which carry access tokens) are never logged; errors keep only Plaid's error type, code and display message. | Plaid has no official .NET SDK, and a thin client is easy to test with a fake handler. |
| 46 | **Access tokens are encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM** (`SecretBox`), keyed by `PLAID_TOKEN_KEY` (32 random bytes, base64, in `.env` or the host's secrets). A wrong key or tampering fails loudly. Tokens are never returned by the API. | CLAUDE.md: encrypt Plaid tokens at rest. Losing the key means re-linking banks, so back it up with your other secrets. |
| 47 | **Refunds never push a category below $0 spent**, in both the Home and Budget calculators. | A mislabeled credit must not inflate Left to Spend. Plaid's sandbox labeled a recurring $810 deposit "Food & Drink", which made Eating out −$2,450. |
| 48 | **Plaid money arriving in checking or savings is income; money arriving on a card is a refund.** Plaid's own categories decide card payments (`LOAN_PAYMENTS_CREDIT_CARD_PAYMENT`), own-account transfers and fees. Transfer detection then runs exactly as for CSVs. | Same reason as 47; card credits really are merchant refunds. |
| 49 | **Pending to posted:** a posted transaction carrying `pending_transaction_id` takes over the pending row in place (amount, date, posted flag and Plaid id change). The row, and any category you set, stays. The pending id's "removed" event then finds nothing. Posted rows are never taken over. | Milestone 3 "done when". Verified against the real Plaid sandbox (6 of 6 posted in place, no duplicates). |
| 50 | **Every page of /transactions/sync is fetched before anything is saved,** then applied in one database transaction together with the new cursor. On `TRANSACTIONS_SYNC_MUTATION_DURING_PAGINATION` it restarts from the saved cursor (up to 3 tries). Syncs of the same item never overlap (a per-item lock). | A failure leaves the previous data and cursor intact. The lock is per process; running more than one server instance would need a database lock. |
| 51 | **Linking a bank to an account with CSV history doesn't duplicate it.** When you link, you choose for each bank account: a new account, an existing one (suggested by matching type and last 4), or skip. In a CSV account, an incoming Plaid transaction with the same amount within 5 days of a CSV row is that row (it gets linked, keeping its category). Unmatched ones are new if they're near or after the CSV's last date, and skipped if they're well inside the CSV's period. | Your year of CSVs plus Plaid's up-to-2-year history would otherwise double count. |
| 52 | **Only checking, savings and credit cards are imported from Plaid;** loans and investments are listed but not imported. Masks keep the last 4 digits only. | Debt accounts and Liabilities come in milestone 5. |
| 53 | **Plaid transaction dates use `authorized_date` when present** (the purchase date), else `date`. | Same rule as card CSVs (decision 22), so CSV and Plaid rows line up. |
| 54 | **Webhooks are verified exactly as Plaid documents:** an ES256 JWT in `Plaid-Verification`, a key looked up by `kid`, issued within 5 minutes, and a matching SHA-256 of the body. Unverified calls get 401. `SYNC_UPDATES_AVAILABLE` queues a background sync; `ITEM` errors and expirations mark the connection. | Nobody can fake a webhook. |
| 55 | **Background sync** is a hosted service: it handles the webhook queue and sweeps every active connection every 6 hours (`Plaid:SweepHours`). Locally Plaid can't reach the webhook URL, so this sweep plus pull-to-refresh keep data fresh. Set `PLAID_WEBHOOK_URL` once hosted. | SPEC section 2 allows a hosted BackgroundService. |
| 56 | **Home shows sync health:** "error" if any connection needs a new login or failed, "stale" if one hasn't synced successfully in 24 hours, with a banner saying so. Pull down (or tap Refresh) to sync now. | SPEC 4.2: say so instead of showing a confident number. |
| 57 | **Disconnecting a bank keeps its accounts and history;** they just stop syncing. "Reconnect" uses Plaid Link's update mode. | Removing history should be an explicit choice. |
| 58 | **Sandbox helpers (Development + `PLAID_ENV=sandbox` only):** "Add Plaid's sandbox test bank" links Plaid's test institution server-side with the `user_transactions_dynamic` test user, so no credentials are typed anywhere. "Simulate new activity" calls `/transactions/refresh`, which makes pending sandbox purchases post. Neither exists in production. | Makes the pending-to-posted path easy to see and test. |
| 59 | **Two levels of Plaid tests:** an in-process fake Plaid runs in every test run (CI included). An opt-in test runs against the real sandbox only when `.env` has sandbox keys, and is skipped in CI. | CI never needs or sees Plaid secrets. |

## CSV and bank sync in either order (follow-up to milestone 3)

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|----------|-----|
| 60 | **Uploading a CSV into an account Plaid already syncs skips the transactions Plaid already has.** A CSV line with the same amount within 5 days of a synced row that no CSV line has claimed yet is the same purchase: it isn't added, and the synced row is stamped with the line's fingerprint, so uploading the same file again is recognized too. The import result reports these separately ("already synced from your bank"). | Before this, CSV uploads only checked earlier CSV uploads, so Plaid-first then CSV double counted. |
| 61 | **Both directions share one rule** (`OverlapMatcher`). The period the other source covers comes from all of its rows in the account, including ones matched earlier. Unmatched lines well inside that period are skipped; ones before it starts, or near its end, are added. This replaces decision 51's "older unmatched are skipped". Plaid history from before your first CSV row is now imported instead of dropped. | One rule, the same in both orders. |
| 62 | **A CSV can't be uploaded into a second, unlinked account for a bank-synced one** (same type and last 4 digits). The message names the synced account to use instead. The account picker labels bank-synced accounts. | The same real card set up twice would count everything twice. |

## Milestone 4: Budget (dashboard, generator, budget months, rollover and carry-forward)

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|----------|-----|
| 65 | **A month's closing number is its final Left to Spend:** unspent minus overspent across spending categories, minus any deficit carried in. A surplus becomes sweep items; a deficit carries into the next month as "Carried over". So a carried deficit is paid back before anything counts as surplus. | Otherwise a deficit would be forgiven by the next month's underspending (which only happened because of the deficit) and the money swept away. |
| 66 | **Months close 5 days after they end** (automatically, on the next visit), or earlier with "Close now". Until then the result is shown as provisional and updates as late CSV or Plaid transactions arrive. After closing, the result, each category's limit and spending, and the sweep items are frozen; later transactions dated in a closed month don't change it. | You import CSVs after the fact, and Plaid can be a few days behind. |
| 67 | **A month's limits are frozen the moment it ends** (at the first visit after its last day). Editing limits "from now on" never rewrites a month that's over; only its spending can still change during the grace period. | Found in testing: applying a new budget changed October's provisional result from $42.50 to $182.50 over. |
| 68 | **Carry-forward starts with the first month the app was used** (the first budget month on record). If the app isn't opened for a while, the missing months are created and closed in order. Imported history from before that doesn't carry anything. | Your budget starts when you start budgeting; history still feeds the generator. |
| 69 | **Sweep items:** "Send $X to …", marked sent by you (the app can't move money). They show on Home and Budget until marked sent. **Debt** goes to the credit card with the largest balance owed for now; once APRs are tracked (milestone 5) it switches to highest APR first, your chosen strategy. **Savings** goes to the first savings account, else the first savings category. **Split** rounds the savings share down to the cent and gives the rest to debt. With no card, everything goes to savings, and vice versa. Set in More → Settings. | SPEC section 3. The payday plan (milestone 9) will list them too. |
| 70 | **Limit changes ask "This month only" or "From now on".** This-month-only is stored per month and tagged on the category; from-now-on changes the usual limit and clears any override for the current month. Hiding a category removes it from the current and future budgets but keeps its history; frozen months keep it. | SPEC 4.3. |
| 71 | **Budget generator:** up to the last 6 complete budget months that have transactions. Medians count months with no spending as $0. Spending limits round up to the dollar, with discretionary ones trimmed 10% while any card has a balance owed. Bills, debt payments and savings are set at their median. Income is the median of paychecks plus partner money. The unallocated remainder is shown, never auto-assigned, with a warning when the plan spends more than income. Applying it sets the usual limits and this month's expected income. A household with no categories gets the standard set at $0. | SPEC 6.1. Debt minimums and savings goals arrive in milestones 5 and 8. Until then debt and savings use what you actually paid. |
| 72 | **Expected income is per month:** a new month starts with the previous month's figure, and it's editable on the Budget tab. The plan shows Income, Planned, and Unallocated (or **Over income** in red). | SPEC section 3: the budget must balance. The pay forecast (milestone 9) will fill this in. |
| 73 | **Demo seed has four months:** August and September closed with surpluses (August's sweep marked sent, September's pending), October $42.50 over (carried into November), and the current month. | Shows every state of the month close; gives the generator history. |

## Planned: budget generator hookups by milestone

The generator (decision 71) ships in milestone 4 using what's available then: past spending, past bill payments, what was actually paid toward cards and savings, and past income including partner deposits. Each later milestone **must rewire its piece of the generator** as part of its own "done when", with a test, so the proposal ends up built from spending needs, bills, debt, partner contributions and the Payday forecast. Every proposed number keeps a plain-language reason.

| Milestone | Generator change | Done when (add to that milestone's checks) |
|---|---|---|
| **5 · Debt** | The debt-payment proposal becomes **the sum of card minimums**. What's left after the starter emergency-fund contribution (milestone 8's priority order) is proposed as extra to the **highest-APR card**. The sweep destination (decision 69) switches from "largest balance" to highest APR. | A test with known balances, APRs and minimums gives minimums + the exact extra on the right card, and the sweep names the highest-APR card. |
| **6 · Bills & subscriptions** | Bill proposals come from **detected recurring items** (actual current amounts, so a price increase is picked up). Subscriptions the bank filed under a spending category are proposed as bills, not spending (closes decision 41's gap). | A test with a recurring charge whose price rose proposes the new price, and a streaming charge labeled "Entertainment" lands in Subscriptions. |
| **7 · Partner** | (Partner money is already tracked separately from income since decision 79.) A fixed contribution **offsets its target category** (Rent shows the full rent, the $800 offset, and your net share). The matched partner deposits are **removed from income**, so partner money is counted once. Split categories propose **your share** only. | A test where Left to Spend and income vs planned come out identical with partner money modeled as an offset instead of income. The section 3 rent ($800 offset) and groceries (50/50) examples reproduce exactly. |
| **8 · Savings** | The savings proposal comes from **goals**: the rainy-day fund's monthly contribution until its ~$1,000 target, then larger savings, following the configurable priority order (starter fund, then high-APR debt, then savings). | A test where the proposal funds the starter goal first, then shifts to debt once it's reached. |
| **9 · Payday** | Expected income for a budget month becomes **the forecast net pay of the checks that land in it** (the 7th and 22nd checks), plus expected partner money where it isn't already an offset. It replaces the historical median and is labeled with its low/likely/high range. Re-importing a schedule re-runs the **section 3 adjustment**: if expected income falls short, cut spending limits (discretionary first, then the rest proportionally) and show every change for approval before it applies. If income rises, don't raise limits; suggest the extra goes to debt. | The Appendix C November case: about $2,533 of pay in the budget month starting 11-06 produces a shortfall, limits are cut discretionary-first with a diff to approve, and a heavier month suggests extra to debt instead of raising limits. |

## Categories from your own transactions, and every category reviewed (requested Nov 2026)

You don't have gas or a phone bill (electric car, someone else's plan), and Walmart is sometimes groceries and sometimes household, so nothing should be premade and nothing should be settled without you.

| # | Decision | Why |
